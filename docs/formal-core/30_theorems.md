# ASM Theorems v1.0

## Purpose

This document defines the mathematical properties derived from the
axioms and definitions of the Formal Core of the Accounting State Model
(ASM).

A theorem is a property that follows from the formal structure of ASM.

---

## Effect Uniqueness Theorem

**Statement:**

Every Event has exactly one Effect.

**Assumptions:**

Let:

$$
e\in\mathcal{E}
$$

**Formal expression:**

$$
\forall e\in\mathcal{E},
\exists!\alpha(e)\in\Delta\mathcal{S}
$$

such that:

$$
\alpha:
\mathcal{E}
\rightarrow
\Delta\mathcal{S}
$$

**Proof sketch:**

By definition, $\alpha$ is a function.

A function assigns exactly one output to each input.

Therefore:

$$
\exists!\alpha(e)
$$

holds.

**Conclusion:**

An Event has a uniquely defined Effect.

---

## Effect and Transition Separation Theorem

**Statement:**

Effect and State Transition are different mathematical concepts.

**Formal expression:**

Effect:

$$
\alpha(e)\in\Delta\mathcal{S}
$$

Transition:

$$
\delta:
\mathcal{S}
\times
\Delta\mathcal{S}
\rightarrow
\mathcal{S}
$$

**Proof sketch:**

Effect describes the change generated by an Event.

Transition describes the operation that applies the change to a State.

Therefore:

$$
\alpha(e)\neq\delta
$$

**Conclusion:**

ASM separates:

$$
what\ changes
$$

from:

$$
how\ the\ change\ is\ applied
$$

## State Transition Determinacy Theorem

**Statement:**

Given a State and an Effect, a deterministic transition produces one
next State.

**Assumptions:**

Let:

$$
S_t\in\mathcal{S}
$$

and:

$$
\alpha(e)\in\Delta\mathcal{S}
$$

**Formal expression:**

$$
\exists!S_{t+1}\in\mathcal{S}
$$

such that:

$$
S_{t+1}
=
\delta(S_t,\alpha(e))
$$

**Proof sketch:**

Since:

$$
\delta:
\mathcal{S}
\times
\Delta\mathcal{S}
\rightarrow
\mathcal{S}
$$

is a function, every input pair:

$$
(S_t,\alpha(e))
$$

has exactly one output.

**Conclusion:**

A deterministic ASM transition has a unique result.

## Event Sequence Reconstruction Theorem

**Statement:**

A sequence of Events can reconstruct the resulting State by applying
Effects in order.

**Assumptions:**

Let:

$$
H(t)=((e_1,t_1),(e_2,t_2),\dots,(e_n,t_n))
$$

and:

$$
S_0\in\mathcal{S}
$$

**Formal expression:**

$$
S_n
=
\delta(
\dots
\delta(
\delta(
S_0,\alpha(e_1)
),
\alpha(e_2)
)
\dots,
\alpha(e_n)
)
$$

**Proof sketch:**

By repeated application of the State Transition Function:

$$
S_{i+1}
=
\delta(S_i,\alpha(e_i))
$$

the final State is obtained.

**Conclusion:**

A History of Events contains sufficient information to derive the State
when the transition rules are known.

## History-State Mapping Theorem

**Statement:**

A State is a derived representation of History.

**Formal expression:**

$$
G:
\mathcal{H}
\rightarrow
\mathcal{S}
$$

such that:

$$
S_t=G(H(t))
$$

**Proof sketch:**

History contains ordered Events.

Each Event produces an Effect.

Each Effect produces a State transition.

Therefore History determines the resulting State under a fixed
transition model.

**Conclusion:**

State is not a primitive object.

State is derived from accumulated Events.

## Multiple Representation Theorem

**Statement:**

The same History may produce different Representations.

**Assumptions:**

Let:

$$
H\in\mathcal{H}
$$

and:

$$
Q_1,Q_2\in\mathcal{Q}
$$

where:

$$
Q_1\neq Q_2
$$

**Formal expression:**

$$
F_{Q_1}:
\mathcal{H}
\rightarrow
\mathcal{Y}_{Q_1}
$$

and:

$$
F_{Q_2}:
\mathcal{H}
\rightarrow
\mathcal{Y}_{Q_2}
$$

such that:

$$
F_{Q_1}(H)
\neq
F_{Q_2}(H)
$$

may hold.

**Proof sketch:**

Each Task defines its own Representation Function.

Therefore the output depends on the selected Task.

**Conclusion:**

There is no single mandatory Representation of History.

---

## Representation Compression Theorem

**Statement:**

A Representation may contain less information than the original History.

**Formal expression:**

There may exist:

$$
H_1,H_2\in\mathcal{H}
$$

such that:

$$
H_1\neq H_2
$$

and:

$$
F_Q(H_1)=F_Q(H_2)
$$

**Proof sketch:**

A Representation maps History into a Task-specific space.

The mapping does not necessarily preserve all information.

**Conclusion:**

Representations are purpose-dependent abstractions.

## Constraint Preservation Theorem

**Statement:**

Valid transitions preserve State constraints.

**Assumptions:**

Let:

$$
C:
\mathcal{S}
\rightarrow
\mathbb{R}
$$

be a Constraint Function.

Assume:

$$
C(S_t)=0
$$

and:

$$
Valid(e)
$$

**Formal expression:**

$$
C(S_{t+1})=0
$$

where:

$$
S_{t+1}
=
\delta(S_t,\alpha(e))
$$

**Proof sketch:**

This follows from the Constraint Preservation Principle.

**Conclusion:**

Constraints act as invariants of valid State transitions.
