# ASM Axioms v1.0

## Purpose

This document defines the fundamental axioms of the Formal Core of the
Accounting State Model (ASM).

The axioms describe the assumptions required for the construction of the
ASM framework.

Accounting-specific interpretations are introduced in higher layers.

---

## Observation Principle

**Statement:**

ASM does not directly operate on Reality.

ASM operates on Evidence obtained through Observation.

**Formal expression:**

$$
Obs\subseteq\mathcal{R}\times\mathcal{Z}
$$

where:

- $\mathcal{R}$ is Reality Space.
- $\mathcal{Z}$ is Evidence Space.
- $Obs$ is the Observation Relation.

**Explanation:**

Reality and Evidence are different mathematical objects.

Therefore:

$$
\exists r\in\mathcal{R},
\exists z_1,z_2\in\mathcal{Z}
$$

such that:

$$
(r,z_1)\in Obs
$$

and:

$$
(r,z_2)\in Obs
$$

may hold.

**Consequence:**

ASM represents observed information, not Reality itself.

---

## Interpretation Principle

**Statement:**

Events are generated by interpreting Evidence according to Policies and
Parameters.

**Formal expression:**

$$
I:
\mathcal{Z}
\times
\Theta
\times
\mathcal{M}
\rightarrow
\mathcal{E}
$$

where:

- $\Theta$ is Policy Space.
- $\mathcal{M}$ is Parameter Space.
- $\mathcal{E}$ is Event Space.

**Explanation:**

The same Evidence may generate different Events depending on the
interpretation context.

Formally:

$$
\exists z\in\mathcal{Z},
\exists\theta_1,\theta_2\in\Theta,
\exists m_1,m_2\in\mathcal{M}
$$

such that:

$$
(\theta_1,m_1)\neq(\theta_2,m_2)
$$

and:

$$
I(z,\theta_1,m_1)
\neq
I(z,\theta_2,m_2)
$$

may hold.

**Consequence:**

Interpretation is context-dependent.

---

## Historical Persistence Principle

**Statement:**

Events are accumulated into an ordered History.

**Formal expression:**

$$
H(t)\in(\mathcal{E}\times T)^*
$$

For:

$$
t_1<t_2
$$

the following condition holds:

$$
H(t_1)\subseteq H(t_2)
$$

**Explanation:**

New Events extend History.

Existing Events are not removed implicitly.

**Consequence:**

Corrections and modifications require explicit Events.

---

## Effect Generation Principle

**Statement:**

Every Event generates a state change.

**Formal expression:**

$$
\forall e\in\mathcal{E},
\exists!\alpha(e)\in\Delta\mathcal{S}
$$

such that:

$$
\alpha:
\mathcal{E}
\rightarrow
\Delta\mathcal{S}
$$

**Explanation:**

An Event is not itself a State.

An Event produces a transformation of State.

The fundamental relation is:

$$
e
\rightarrow
\alpha(e)
\rightarrow
\Delta S
$$

**Consequence:**

Effects are the bridge between Events and States.

---

## State Transition Principle

**Statement:**

A State transition is generated by applying an Effect caused by an Event.

**Formal expression:**

$$
\forall e\in\mathcal{E},
\forall S_t\in\mathcal{S}
$$

there exists:

$$
S_{t+1}\in\mathcal{S}
$$

such that:

$$
S_{t+1}
=
\delta(S_t,e)
$$

**Explanation:**

The transition function determines how Events transform the current
State.

**Consequence:**

ASM is a state transition model.

---

## Representation Principle

**Statement:**

A History can be transformed into different representations according to
different Tasks.

**Formal expression:**

$$
\forall Q\in\mathcal{Q},
\exists F_Q
$$

such that:

$$
F_Q:
\mathcal{H}
\rightarrow
\mathcal{Y}_Q
$$

**Explanation:**

The same History may generate different representations.

For:

$$
Q_1,Q_2\in\mathcal{Q}
$$

with:

$$
Q_1\neq Q_2
$$

it is possible that:

$$
F_{Q_1}(H)
\neq
F_{Q_2}(H)
$$

**Consequence:**

A single underlying History can support multiple views.

---

## Constraint Preservation Principle

**Statement:**

Valid State transitions preserve required constraints.

**Formal expression:**

$$
\forall S_t\in\mathcal{S},
\forall e\in\mathcal{E}
$$

if:

$$
C(S_t)=0
$$

and:

$$
Valid(e)
$$

then:

$$
C(S_{t+1})=0
$$

where:

$$
S_{t+1}=\delta(S_t,e)
$$

**Explanation:**

Constraints define properties that must remain invariant during valid
transitions.

**Consequence:**

System consistency is maintained through invariant preservation.

---

## Axiom Summary

| Axiom | Main Concept |
| --- | --- |
| Observation Principle | Reality is accessed through Evidence |
| Interpretation Principle | Evidence is transformed into Events |
| Historical Persistence Principle | Events form persistent History |
| Effect Generation Principle | Events generate Effects |
| State Transition Principle | Effects transform States |
| Representation Principle | History generates Task-dependent views |
| Constraint Preservation Principle | Valid transitions preserve invariants |
