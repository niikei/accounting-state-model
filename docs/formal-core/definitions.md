# ASM Definitions v2.0

## Purpose

This document defines the formal concepts of the Accounting State Model
(ASM).

The definitions specify the mathematical objects and relationships used in
the Formal Core.

---

## Reality

**Definition:**

Reality is the domain in which economic activities and changes exist
before accounting interpretation.

**Symbol:**

$$
\mathcal{R}
$$

**Notes:**

Reality exists independently from accounting systems.

$$
Reality\neq Accounting
$$

---

## Evidence

**Definition:**

Evidence is observed information obtained from Reality and used for
accounting interpretation.

**Symbol:**

$$
\mathcal{Z}
$$

**Formal expression:**

$$
Obs\subseteq\mathcal{R}\times\mathcal{Z}
$$

**Examples:**

- Invoice
- Contract
- Bank statement
- Delivery record

**Notes:**

Evidence is an observation of Reality, not Reality itself.

$$
Evidence\neq Reality
$$

---

## Economic Event

**Definition:**

An Economic Event is a meaningful change occurring in Reality.

It represents what happened in the economic world before accounting
recognition.

**Symbol:**

$$
\epsilon
$$

**Formal expression:**

$$
\epsilon\in\mathcal{E}_{econ}
$$

**Examples:**

- Sale of goods
- Acquisition of equipment
- Borrowing money
- Consumption of resources

---

## Accounting Event

**Definition:**

An Accounting Event is an Economic Event interpreted and recognized
within an accounting system.

It is the fundamental unit recorded in Accounting History.

**Symbol:**

$$
e
$$

**Formal expression:**

$$
e\in\mathcal{E}_{acc}
$$

**Generation:**

$$
\widehat{\mathsf{Acc}}_{\theta}(z,\eta)=e
$$

where:

- $z\in\mathcal{Z}$ is Evidence.
- $\eta\in H_{\eta}$ is an Estimate.
- $\theta\in\Theta$ is Accounting Policy.

**Notes:**

Economic Event and Accounting Event are different objects.

$$
\epsilon\neq e
$$

---

## Accounting Policy

**Definition:**

Accounting Policy is the rule system used to determine accounting
recognition and measurement.

**Symbol:**

$$
\theta
$$

**Formal expression:**

$$
\theta\in\Theta
$$

**Examples:**

- IFRS
- GAAP
- Internal accounting rules

---

## Estimate

**Definition:**

Estimate is an assumption representing uncertain values or future
outcomes used during accounting interpretation.

**Symbol:**

$$
\eta
$$

**Formal expression:**

$$
\eta\in H_{\eta}
$$

**Examples:**

- Useful life of assets
- Expected credit loss
- Provision estimates

---

## Accounting Interpretation

**Definition:**

Accounting Interpretation is the transformation process that converts
Evidence into Accounting Events using Accounting Policy and Estimates.

**Symbol:**

$$
\widehat{\mathsf{Acc}}_{\theta}
$$

**Formal expression:**

$$
\widehat{\mathsf{Acc}}_{\theta}:
\mathcal{Z}\times H_{\eta}
\rightarrow
\mathcal{E}_{acc}
$$

---

## Accounting Event History

**Definition:**

Accounting Event History is an ordered sequence of Accounting Events with
associated time information.

**Symbol:**

$$
H_A(t)
$$

**Formal expression:**

$$
H_A(t)
=
((e_1,t_1),(e_2,t_2),\dots,(e_n,t_n))
$$

where:

$$
e_i\in\mathcal{E}_{acc}
$$

and:

$$
t_i\in T
$$

**Notes:**

History preserves event ordering.

$$
(e_1,t_1),(e_2,t_2)
\neq
(e_2,t_2),(e_1,t_1)
$$

---

## Provenance

**Definition:**

Provenance records the origin and supporting basis of an Accounting
Event.

**Symbol:**

$$
Prov
$$

**Formal expression:**

$$
Prov:
\mathcal{E}_{acc}
\rightarrow
2^{\mathcal{Z}\times\Theta\times H_{\eta}}
$$

**Notes:**

Provenance explains why an Accounting Event exists.

$$
Provenance\neq Validity
$$

---

## Accounting Dimension Space

**Definition:**

Accounting Dimension Space represents all dimensions affected by
Accounting Events.

**Symbol:**

$$
\mathcal{X}
$$

**Formal expression:**

$$
\mathcal{X}
=
\mathcal{A}
\times
\mathcal{L}
\times
\mathcal{E}
\times
\mathcal{P}
\times
\mathcal{C}
$$

where:

- $\mathcal{A}$ represents Assets.
- $\mathcal{L}$ represents Liabilities.
- $\mathcal{E}$ represents Equity.
- $\mathcal{P}$ represents Performance.
- $\mathcal{C}$ represents Cash.

---

## Accounting Effect

**Definition:**

Accounting Effect is the change vector generated by an Accounting Event
in Accounting Dimension Space.

**Symbol:**

$$
\alpha(e)
$$

**Formal expression:**

$$
\alpha:
\mathcal{E}_{acc}
\rightarrow
\Delta\mathcal{X}
$$

or:

$$
\alpha(e)
=
(\Delta A,\Delta L,\Delta E,\Delta\Pi,\Delta\Phi_C)
$$

**Meaning:**

Each Accounting Event produces changes in one or more accounting
dimensions.

**Example:**

Credit sale:

$$
\Delta AR>0
$$

$$
\Delta\Pi>0
$$

$$
\Delta\Phi_C=0
$$

---

## Representation

**Definition:**

Representation is a Task-dependent transformation from Accounting
History into an information structure.

**Symbol:**

$$
F_Q
$$

**Formal expression:**

$$
F_Q:
\mathcal{H}_{acc}
\rightarrow
Y_Q
$$

where:

- $Q$ is a Task.
- $Y_Q$ is the representation space for Task $Q$.

**Examples:**

Financial reporting:

$$
F_{financial}(H_A)
$$

Management accounting:

$$
F_{management}(H_A)
$$

Operational management:

$$
F_{operation}(H_A)
$$

---

## Task

**Definition:**

Task is the purpose that determines what information is required.

**Symbol:**

$$
Q
$$

**Formal expression:**

$$
Q\in\mathcal{Q}
$$

**Examples:**

- Prepare financial statements
- Analyze profitability
- Forecast cash requirements

---

## Representation Sufficiency

**Definition:**

A Representation is sufficient for Task $Q$ when the required Task output
can be derived from that Representation.

**Formal expression:**

$$
F_Q\succeq Q
\iff
\exists R,\ Q=R\circ F_Q
$$

---

## Operational State

**Definition:**

Operational State is a Task-dependent compression of Accounting History
that preserves information required for future transitions.

**Symbol:**

$$
S_t
$$

**Formal expression:**

$$
S_t=G_Q(H_A(t))
$$

**Transition:**

$$
S_{t+1}
=
\delta_Q(S_t,e)
$$

**Notes:**

State and Position are different concepts.

$$
State\neq Position
$$

---

## Quantitative Position

**Definition:**

Quantitative Position is a projection of accumulated Accounting Effects
along a selected accounting dimension.

**Symbol:**

$$
P_k(t)
$$

**Formal expression:**

$$
P_k(t)
=
P_k(0)
+
\sum_{e\in H_A(t)}
\alpha_k(e)
$$

where:

$$
k\in\{A,L,E,C\}
$$

**Examples:**

- Asset position
- Liability position
- Equity position
- Cash position

---

## Financial Position

**Definition:**

Financial Position is the accounting representation of Assets,
Liabilities, and Equity.

**Symbol:**

$$
FP_t
$$

**Formal expression:**

$$
FP_t=(A_t,L_t,E_t)
$$

**Constraint:**

$$
A_t-L_t-E_t=0
$$

---

## Performance Flow

**Definition:**

Performance Flow is the accumulated performance effect during a period.

**Symbol:**

$$
\Pi(I)
$$

**Formal expression:**

$$
\Pi(I)
=
\sum_{e\in H_I}
\alpha_{\Pi}(e)
$$

---

## Cash Flow

**Definition:**

Cash Flow is the accumulated cash effect during a period.

**Symbol:**

$$
\Phi_C(I)
$$

**Formal expression:**

$$
\Phi_C(I)
=
\sum_{e\in H_I}
\alpha_C(e)
$$

---

## Book Representation

**Definition:**

Book Representation is a representation of Accounting History using
double-entry records.

**Symbol:**

$$
B
$$

---

## Double Entry Constraint

**Definition:**

Double Entry Constraint is the structural consistency condition applied
to Book Representation.

**Symbol:**

$$
\mathsf{Bal}_B
$$

**Formal expression:**

$$
\mathsf{Bal}_B(B)=0
$$

---

## Validity

**Definition:**

Validity describes whether a Representation satisfies required criteria.

ASM defines three validity dimensions.

---

## Structural Validity

**Definition:**

Structural Validity evaluates formal consistency.

**Example:**

$$
Debit=Credit
$$

---

## Semantic Validity

**Definition:**

Semantic Validity evaluates consistency with accounting meaning and rules.

---

## Empirical Validity

**Definition:**

Empirical Validity evaluates consistency with Reality and Evidence.

---

## Valid Transition

**Definition:**

A Valid Transition is a transition caused by an Accounting Event that
preserves required constraints.

**Formal expression:**

$$
\forall e\in\mathcal{E}_{acc},
\forall t\in T
$$

$$
Valid(e)
\land
C(S_t)=0
\Rightarrow
C(S_{t+1})=0
$$
