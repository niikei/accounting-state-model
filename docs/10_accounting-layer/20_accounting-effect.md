# ASM Accounting Effect v1.0

## Purpose

This document defines Accounting Effects introduced by the Accounting
Layer of ASM.

An Accounting Effect represents the change in Accounting State generated
by an Accounting Event.

The Accounting Effect provides the mathematical foundation of accounting
entries.

---

## Accounting Effect Function

**Definition:**

An Accounting Effect Function maps an Accounting Event to a change in
Accounting State.

**Symbol:**

$$
\alpha_{acc}
$$

**Formal expression:**

$$
\alpha_{acc}:
\mathcal{E}_{acc}
\rightarrow
\Delta\mathcal{S}_{acc}
$$

---

## Accounting Effect

**Definition:**

An Accounting Effect is the state change generated by an Accounting
Event.

**Formal expression:**

$$
\alpha_{acc}(e_{acc})
\in
\Delta\mathcal{S}_{acc}
$$

The relationship is:

$$
Accounting\ Event
\rightarrow
Accounting\ Effect
$$

---

## Effect Vector

The Accounting Effect is represented as:

$$
\alpha_{acc}(e_{acc})
=
(
\Delta A,
\Delta L,
\Delta E,
\Delta\Pi,
\Delta\Phi_C
)
$$

where:

- $\Delta A$ represents change in Asset position.
- $\Delta L$ represents change in Liability position.
- $\Delta E$ represents change in Equity position.
- $\Delta\Pi$ represents change in Performance.
- $\Delta\Phi_C$ represents change in Cash Flow.

---

## Effect Constraint

Every valid Accounting Effect must preserve the Accounting Constraint.

For an Accounting State:

$$
S_{acc,t}
=
(A_t,L_t,E_t,\Pi_t,\Phi_{C,t})
$$

and an Accounting Effect:

$$
\alpha_{acc}(e_{acc})
=
(\Delta A,\Delta L,\Delta E,\Delta\Pi,\Delta\Phi_C)
$$

the resulting State is:

$$
S_{acc,t+1}
=
\delta_{acc}
(
S_{acc,t},
\alpha_{acc}(e_{acc})
)
$$

and must satisfy:

$$
\kappa_{acc}(S_{acc,t+1})=0
$$

---

## Example: Cash Sale

Consider an Accounting Event:

$$
e_{sale}\in\mathcal{E}_{acc}
$$

representing:

"An entity receives 100 units of cash by selling goods."

The Accounting Effect is:

$$
\alpha_{acc}(e_{sale})
=
(
+100Cash,
+100Revenue
)
$$

In the Accounting State representation:

$$
\Delta A=+100
$$

and:

$$
\Delta\Pi=+100
$$

The Accounting State changes as:

$$
S_{acc,t+1}
=
\delta_{acc}
(
S_{acc,t},
\alpha_{acc}(e_{sale})
)
$$

---

## Relation to Double Entry

A traditional journal entry represents an Accounting Effect using
Debit/Credit notation.

The Debit/Credit representation is therefore:

$$
Representation:
\Delta\mathcal{S}_{acc}
\rightarrow
Journal
$$

The Journal Entry is not the Accounting Effect itself.

It is a representation of the Accounting Effect.

---

## Interpretation

ASM separates three concepts:

| Concept | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Accounting Event | What happened |
| Accounting Effect | What changed |
| Journal Entry | How the change is recorded |

The fundamental structure is:

$$
Event
\rightarrow
Effect
\rightarrow
State
$$

and:

$$
Effect
\rightarrow
Journal\ Representation
$$

---

## Consequence

A Journal Entry is not the source of accounting meaning.

The source is the Accounting Effect generated by an Accounting Event.

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
Journal\ Entry
\neq
Accounting\ Effect
}
$$

A Journal Entry is a representation of an Accounting Effect.
