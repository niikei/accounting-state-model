# ASM Definitions v1.2

## Purpose

This document defines the fundamental concepts of the Formal Core of the
Accounting State Model (ASM).

The definitions specify the mathematical objects used in ASM.

Domain-specific concepts are introduced in higher layers.

---

## Reality

**Definition:**

Reality is the domain containing objects and changes that exist
independently of the ASM model.

**Symbol:**

$$
\mathcal{R}
$$

**Meaning:**

Reality is the source from which Evidence is obtained.

ASM does not directly operate on Reality.

---

## Evidence

**Definition:**

Evidence is observable information obtained from Reality.

**Symbol:**

$$
\mathcal{Z}
$$

**Formal relationship:**

$$
Obs\subseteq\mathcal{R}\times\mathcal{Z}
$$

**Meaning:**

Evidence is not Reality itself.

$$
\mathcal{Z}\neq\mathcal{R}
$$

Evidence represents an observation of Reality.

---

## Interpretation

**Definition:**

Interpretation is the transformation process that converts Evidence into
Events according to Policies and Parameters.

**Symbol:**

$$
I
$$

**Formal expression:**

$$
I:
\mathcal{Z}
\times
\Theta
\times
\mathcal{M}
\rightarrow
\mathcal{E}
$$

**Meaning:**

Interpretation determines how observed information is represented as an
Event.

---

## Policy

**Definition:**

Policy is a rule system used during Interpretation.

**Symbol:**

$$
\theta
$$

**Formal expression:**

$$
\theta\in\Theta
$$

**Meaning:**

Policy determines the rules applied when generating Events.

---

## Parameter

**Definition:**

Parameter is a value or assumption used during Interpretation.

**Symbol:**

$$
m
$$

**Formal expression:**

$$
m\in\mathcal{M}
$$

**Meaning:**

Parameters provide additional information required for Interpretation.

---

## Event

**Definition:**

An Event is an occurrence represented in ASM that generates an Effect.

**Symbol:**

$$
e
$$

**Formal expression:**

$$
e\in\mathcal{E}
$$

**Meaning:**

An Event represents the causal input of a State transition.

An Event itself is not a State change.

Therefore:

$$
Event
\neq
Effect
$$

---

## History

**Definition:**

History is an ordered sequence of Events and their associated Times.

**Symbol:**

$$
H_t
$$

**Formal expression:**

$$
H_t\in(\mathcal{E}\times T)^*
$$

or:

$$
H_t
=
((e_1,t_1),(e_2,t_2),\dots,(e_n,t_n))
$$

where:

$$
e_i\in\mathcal{E}
$$

and:

$$
t_i\in T
$$

**Meaning:**

History records the sequence of Events accumulated up to time $t$.

History is the persistent source from which State can be reconstructed.

---

## Effect

**Definition:**

Effect is an element of the State change space generated by an Event.

**Symbol:**

$$
\alpha(e)
$$

**Formal expression:**

$$
\alpha:
\mathcal{E}
\rightarrow
\Delta\mathcal{S}
$$

and:

$$
\alpha(e)\in\Delta\mathcal{S}
$$

**Meaning:**

Effect represents what changes when an Event occurs.

The relationship is:

$$
Event
\rightarrow
Effect
$$

Effect is not the resulting State.

---

## Flow

**Definition:**

Flow is an accumulation of Effects over a temporal interval.

**Symbol:**

$$
\mathcal{F}
$$

**Formal expression:**

For a period:

$$
\tau\subseteq T
$$

Flow is:

$$
Flow(\tau)
=
\sum_{(e,t)\in H_\tau}
\alpha(e)
$$

**Meaning:**

Effect represents an instantaneous State change.

Flow represents accumulated changes over time.

Therefore:

$$
Effect
\neq
Flow
$$

---

## State Space

**Definition:**

State Space is the set of all possible conditions of the modeled system.

**Symbol:**

$$
\mathcal{S}
$$

**Meaning:**

State Space defines the possible values that a State may take.

---

## State

**Definition:**

State is the condition of the modeled system at a specific Time.

**Symbol:**

$$
S_t
$$

**Formal expression:**

$$
S_t\in\mathcal{S}
$$

**Meaning:**

State represents the information required to describe the current system
condition.

State is derived from accumulated History.

---

## State Transition

**Definition:**

State Transition is the process of applying an Effect to a State and
producing a new State.

**Symbol:**

$$
\delta
$$

**Formal expression:**

$$
\delta:
\mathcal{S}
\times
\Delta\mathcal{S}
\rightarrow
\mathcal{S}
$$

such that:

$$
S_{t+1}
=
\delta(S_t,\alpha(e))
$$

**Meaning:**

The Transition Function determines how an Effect changes the current
State.

---

## State Generation

**Definition:**

State Generation is the transformation from History into State
under a fixed Initial State, Effect Function, and Transition Function.

**Symbol:**

$$
G_{S_0,\alpha,\delta}
$$

**Formal expression:**

$$
G_{S_0,\alpha,\delta}:
\mathcal{H}
\rightarrow
\mathcal{S}
$$

such that:

$$
S_t
=
G_{S_0,\alpha,\delta}(H_t)
$$

**Meaning:**

State is generated from accumulated History under fixed transition rules.

Different Histories may generate the same State.

Therefore:

$$
History
\neq
State
$$

---

## Representation

**Definition:**

Representation is a transformation of ASM information into information
suitable for a specific Purpose.

**Symbol:**

$$
F_Q
$$

**Formal expression:**

$$
F_Q:
\mathcal{X}_Q
\rightarrow
\mathcal{Y}_Q
$$

where:

$$
Q\in\mathcal{Q}
$$

**Meaning:**

Representation generates a purpose-dependent view of the underlying
system.

Representation is not identical to State.

---

## Task

**Definition:**

Task is the Purpose that determines the required information.

**Symbol:**

$$
Q
$$

**Formal expression:**

$$
Q\in\mathcal{Q}
$$

**Meaning:**

Different Tasks may require different Representations.

---

## Constraint

**Definition:**

Constraint is a condition that defines valid States.

**Symbol:**

$$
\kappa
$$

**Formal expression:**

$$
\kappa:
\mathcal{S}
\rightarrow
\mathbb{R}
$$

A valid State satisfies:

$$
\kappa(S_t)=0
$$

**Meaning:**

Constraints define invariant properties that must be preserved.

---

## Valid Transition

**Definition:**

A Valid Transition is a State Transition that preserves required
Constraints.

**Formal expression:**

If:

$$
\kappa(S_t)=0
$$

and:

$$
S_{t+1}
=
\delta(S_t,\alpha(e))
$$

then:

$$
\kappa(S_{t+1})=0
$$

**Meaning:**

Valid Events and Effects preserve system consistency.

---

## Formal Core Structure

ASM contains two related structures.

### Information Preservation Structure

$$
\mathcal{R}
\rightarrow
\mathcal{Z}
\rightarrow
\mathcal{E}
\rightarrow
\mathcal{H}
\rightarrow
\mathcal{S}
\rightarrow
\mathcal{Y}_Q
$$

---

### State Transition Structure

$$
\mathcal{E}
\rightarrow
\Delta\mathcal{S}
\rightarrow
\mathcal{S}
$$

The mathematical transition is:

$$
e
\rightarrow
\alpha(e)
\rightarrow
S_{t+1}
$$
